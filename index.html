How YouTube income per 1,000 views is calculated

Creators earn money from ads shown on their videos. The number that matters is RPM (revenue per mille): what you earn for every 1,000 views after YouTube takes its cut. It is different from CPM, which is what advertisers pay before the split.

Earnings = (Views ÷ 1,000) × RPM

For example, 250,000 views at a $4 RPM is 250 × $4 = $1,000. Your RPM moves with four things: the niche (advertisers pay more for finance than for music), the country of your viewers, the video type (Shorts pay far less), and the time of year (Q4 ad budgets lift RPM).

CPM vs RPM

CPM is the advertiser’s price per 1,000 ad impressions. RPM is your take-home per 1,000 video views. RPM is almost always lower, because not every view shows an ad and YouTube keeps a share.

YouTube’s revenue share

For long-form videos YouTube’s published split gives creators 55% of ad revenue. Shorts use a pooled system, which is why their payout per view is a fraction of long video.

Earning from India

Indian viewers are plentiful but pay roughly a tenth of US rates per view. Creators in India often grow income by making content in niches with higher ad value (finance, tech, education) or by attracting English-speaking US, UK and Gulf viewers.

YouTube income per 1000 views in India

If you upload from India, the first thing to know is that YouTube pays by where your viewers are, not where you live. A creator in Mumbai whose audience is mostly in India earns far less per view than a creator in Delhi whose videos are watched in the US. For most Indian-audience channels, YouTube income per 1000 views in India is roughly ₹20 to ₹150 for long videos, which is about $0.25 to $1.75. Entertainment, music and kids channels sit at the bottom of that range, while finance, business and tech channels reach the top or go beyond it.

The reason is simple. Advertisers pay more to reach people with higher purchasing power in their market, so an ad shown in the US costs the advertiser much more than the same ad shown in India. That gap is why the calculator above applies a factor of about 0.10 to Indian audiences. Indian viewers are valuable in volume, but each view carries a small price tag.

How much does YouTube pay for 1 lakh views in India?

Using the range above, 1 lakh (100,000) views usually brings in about ₹2,000 to ₹15,000 on long videos. A general vlog channel will be near the low end, while a stock market or personal finance channel can pass the high end. At 10 lakh views the same channel would earn roughly ₹20,000 to ₹1,50,000. These are estimates, not promises, and your own YouTube Studio revenue report is the only exact number.

Why finance and tech pay more in India too

Advertisers on a stock broker, credit card or online course have a high customer value, so they bid more for those viewers. An Indian finance channel can therefore earn several times more per 1,000 views than an Indian comedy channel with the same audience size. If your goal is income and you can choose, topic choice matters more than almost anything else you can control.

YouTube Shorts income per 1000 views

Shorts earn much less per view than long videos. A typical figure is $0.02 to $0.08 per 1,000 views for a US audience, which means a Short with one million views might earn only $20 to $80. For an Indian audience the amount is roughly a tenth of that, often a few rupees per 1,000 views. Shorts are viewed in a fast-scrolling feed with fewer ads, and the revenue is pooled and shared, which keeps the payout per view low.

This does not make Shorts useless. They are one of the quickest ways to find new viewers, and a well-timed Short can send people to your long videos where the real money is made. Many creators treat Shorts as marketing and long-form as the product.

How much does YouTube pay per 1000 views in the USA?

For a US audience, long-form videos commonly earn $1 to $10 per 1,000 views, depending on the niche, with finance and business reaching $10 to $30. A tech channel with 100,000 monthly views from American viewers might expect about $400 to $1,100. These figures are higher than anywhere else because US advertiser budgets are the largest in the world. If you can create content that appeals to US, UK, Canadian and Australian viewers, your income per view can rise sharply even with the same view count.

YouTube RPM vs CPM: which number matters?

CPM is what an advertiser pays for 1,000 ad impressions. RPM is what you take home for 1,000 video views after YouTube’s 45% share of long-form ad revenue is removed. Because not every view shows an ad, RPM is always lower than CPM. When people ask about income per 1,000 views, RPM is the figure they mean, and it is the one this calculator estimates.

How many views to make $1,000 or ₹1 lakh

At a $4 RPM you need about 250,000 views to earn $1,000. At $10 RPM it drops to 100,000 views. For Indian audiences, earning ₹1 lakh at a ₹60 RPM takes around 16 lakh views. Enter your own numbers in the calculator and the “Views to earn” box shows your exact target.

Things that change your income per 1,000 views

Four factors do most of the work: your niche, your audience’s country, the video format and the season. Ad prices rise in the last quarter of the year when brands spend on holiday and festival campaigns, which is why many creators see higher RPM in October to December and a dip in January. Video length also matters, since videos over eight minutes can carry mid-roll ads. Finally, advertiser-friendly content keeps your RPM healthy, while sensitive topics can trigger limited ads and lower pay.

All figures in this guide are estimates based on commonly reported creator RPMs and are not official YouTube data.

How to raise your income per 1,000 views

  • Choose a higher-value topic. The same views earn several times more in finance or business than in entertainment.
  • Make videos over 8 minutes. Longer videos can carry mid-roll ads, which lifts RPM.
  • Attract viewers from higher-paying countries. Use clear English titles and topics that interest US and UK audiences.
  • Keep content advertiser-friendly. Limited ads from sensitive topics cut RPM sharply.
  • Add other income. Memberships, affiliate links and sponsorships often earn more than ads per view.

To earn ad revenue you must join the YouTube Partner Program. Check YouTube Help for the current subscriber and watch-time requirements.

Frequently asked questions

How much does YouTube pay per 1000 views?

Most long-form channels earn between about $1 and $10 per 1,000 views, but it ranges from under $1 for music or kids content to $20+ for finance. Shorts usually pay roughly $0.02 to $0.08 per 1,000 views.

What is the difference between CPM and RPM?

CPM is what advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions. RPM is what you earn per 1,000 video views after YouTube’s share is removed. RPM is the number that matters for your income.

How much does YouTube pay per 1000 views in India?

Indian audiences typically produce an RPM of roughly 10% of US rates, often around ₹20 to ₹150 per 1,000 views for long videos, depending on the niche.

How many views do I need to make $1,000?

At a $4 RPM you need about 250,000 views. At $10 RPM you need 100,000 views. The calculator above shows the figure for your niche and country.

Why do Shorts pay less than long videos?

Shorts play in a fast feed with fewer ads, and revenue is pooled and shared among creators, so the payout per 1,000 views is much lower.

Are these numbers exact?
